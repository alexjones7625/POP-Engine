## HEADER & HERO SECTION

### ES-001: Header Structure Framework

**The Rule**: Structure your email header with: Logo > Visual > Tagline/Headline > Supporting text > CTA

**Why It Works**: Creates a logical flow that guides the eye from brand recognition to action. The sequence builds familiarity first, then delivers value, then requests action.

**Application**:
1. Logo at top for brand recognition
2. Strong visual/hero image
3. Clever, creative headline unique to your audience
4. Brief text elaborating on the big idea (2-3 sentences max)
5. Clear CTA button

**Example**: Allbirds email - Logo > Product image > "Take it outside" headline > Brief copy > Shop CTA

---

### ES-007: No Navigation in Header

**The Rule**: Never put navigation links (Shop Men, Shop Women, etc.) at the TOP of emails. Move them to footer only.

**Why It Works**: Top navigation creates decision fatigue immediately. The email hasn't made its case yet. The reader hasn't been convinced of anything and you're asking them to navigate away.

**Application**:
- Remove header nav links entirely
- Let the email lead with its message
- Put navigation options in the footer for those who scroll through

**Anti-Pattern**:
```
BAD: [Logo] | Shop Men | Shop Women | New Arrivals | Sale
```
```
GOOD: [Logo only]
       [Hero image + headline]
```

## BODY STRUCTURE

### ES-002: Two-to-Three Sections Maximum

**The Rule**: Limit emails to 2-3 content sections maximum. Each section = Content + CTA.

**Why It Works**: Gmail and other email apps truncate long emails. Shorter emails get fully seen and have higher engagement. More sections means more places to lose attention.

**Application**:
- Section 1: Header + hero (content + CTA)
- Section 2: Supporting content/proof (content + CTA)
- Section 3: Footer
- No more than 3 content blocks before footer

**Structure Example**:
```
Section 1: Product hero + shop CTA
Section 2: Social proof/reviews + shop CTA
Footer: Categories, social, unsubscribe
```

---

### ES-009: 1-2 Scroll Maximum

**The Rule**: Your email should not be longer than 1-2 scrolls maximum on mobile.

**Why It Works**: Length correlates directly with abandonment. Every additional scroll is a chance to lose the reader. Shorter = higher probability of reading the entire message.

**Application**:
- Ruthlessly cut until your email fits within 1-2 mobile screen scrolls
- Test on actual mobile device before sending
- If content requires more space, split into multiple emails

**Example**: Seed probiotic email fits entire pitch in one scroll with benefit icons.

---

### ES-005: Visual Over Text Rule

**The Rule**: Use visual elements to explain concepts instead of text whenever possible.

**Why It Works**: The brain processes images 60,000 times faster than words. Visuals communicate instantly and are more memorable.

**Application**:
- Replace text-heavy explanations with comparison graphics
- Use icons to represent benefits
- Convert feature lists into illustrated benefits
- Use infographics instead of paragraphs

**Example**: Magic Spoon uses a visual side-by-side comparison chart (Magic Spoon vs "The Other Guys") instead of listing differences in text.

---

### ES-018: Product Grid Layout Variation

**The Rule**: Vary product grid layouts (1 column, 2 column, offset) within the same email and across emails.

**Why It Works**: Predictable layouts are boring. Visual variety releases dopamine and keeps subscribers scrolling. Pattern breaks maintain attention.

**Application**:
- Don't just stack 2x3 grids repeatedly
- Mix: 1-product hero section, 2-column row, offset layouts
- Alternate between full-width and grid displays

**Layout Options**:
```
Full-width hero product
2-column side by side
3-column grid
Offset/staggered
Single product feature
```

---

### ES-019: Transition Gradients Between Sections

**The Rule**: Use gradient transitions between email sections instead of hard color breaks.

**Why It Works**: Creates immersive, smooth scrolling experience. Feels like one cohesive piece, not stacked blocks.

**Application**: Add subtle gradient fade from one background color to the next at section boundaries.

---

### ES-020: Product Images Breaking Frames

**The Rule**: Have product images or models extend beyond/overlap their background boxes.

**Why It Works**: Creates visual depth and immersion. Makes flat email feel 3D and dynamic. Stops the scroll because it's unexpected.

**Application**: Remove backgrounds from product photos, layer them to overlap borders and sections.

## CTA PLACEMENT

### ES-003: CTA Above the Fold

**The Rule**: Place your primary CTA where it's visible without scrolling.

**Why It Works**: Emails with CTA above the fold consistently get higher click-through rates. Reduces friction - no scroll required to act. Captures impulse clickers immediately.

**Application**:
- Design your email so the first CTA button is visible in the initial viewport
- Test on both desktop and mobile
- Don't make readers work to find the action

**Example**: Magic Spoon places "Be Extraordinary" CTA button visible immediately without scrolling.

---

### ES-021: Individual Shop Buttons Per Product

**The Rule**: Every product shown MUST have its own "Shop Now" button or underlined product name link.

**Why It Works**: Customers don't know images are clickable. Explicit CTAs increase clicks significantly. Removing ambiguity drives action.

**Application**:
- Add button or underlined text link below every product in a grid
- Never assume the image click will be discovered
- Label every clickable product clearly

---

### ES-004: One Destination Per Email

**The Rule**: Each email should drive to a single destination/objective. Don't split traffic between multiple CTAs with different destinations.

**Why It Works**: Multiple destinations dilute attention and split your traffic. Some clicks go to low-value destinations (like Instagram) instead of purchase pages.

**Application**:
- If promoting a product, all CTAs go to that product
- Save other promotions for separate emails
- Never compete with yourself for clicks

**Anti-Pattern**: Dr. Squatch email promoting a product also had a giveaway CTA leading to Instagram - this splits traffic and wastes qualified email subscribers on low-conversion destinations.

## FOOTER STRUCTURE

### ES-006: Footer Framework

**The Rule**: Footer structure: Product categories (optional) > Contact info > Rewards program link > Social icons > Business address > Manage preferences/Unsubscribe

**Why It Works**: Organized footers project professionalism. Easy unsubscribe prevents spam complaints and resentment. Clean structure builds trust.

**Application**:
- Keep footer clean and simple
- Never hide the unsubscribe link - hidden unsubscribes create angry customers
- Standard order creates familiarity

**Example Structure**:
```
[Logo]
[Tagline]
[Social icons]
[Address]
[Manage preferences | Unsubscribe]
```

---

### ES-008: Shop By Category Footer

**The Rule**: Every email should end with shop-by-category buttons before the footer.

**Why It Works**: If someone scrolls to the bottom without finding what they want, give them an exit ramp to browse categories. Prevents complete bounce.

**Application**: Add 3-5 category buttons above the unsubscribe footer

**Example**:
```
[Shop Women] [Shop Men] [New Arrivals] [Sale]
---
[Footer with unsubscribe]
```

---

### ES-012: Social Media Icon Warning

**The Rule**: Consider removing social media links from email footers, or test their impact.

**Why It Works**: Email subscribers are high-intent qualified traffic. Sending them to social media means they get lost in the algorithm, forget your email, and don't complete the purchase.

**Application**:
- A/B test emails with and without social icons
- Track if removing them increases conversions
- If you keep them, place them low priority

**Example Flow**: User clicks Instagram icon > Gets lost in feed for 1-2 hours > Forgets email and intended action.

---

### ES-013: Rewards Program Visibility

**The Rule**: If you have a rewards program, display it prominently in emails with personalized point balances.

**Why It Works**: Showing earned points creates a sense of value and encourages redemption through purchases. Personalization increases engagement.

**Application**:
- Place rewards bar below logo showing subscriber's current points
- Add "Redeem" CTA
- Use dynamic content that syncs with loyalty program (e.g., Klaviyo integration)

**Example**: Dr. Squatch shows personalized point balance at top of email with redemption CTA.

## SUBJECT LINE & PREVIEW RULES

### ES-010: Subject Line Length Rule

**The Rule**: Keep subject lines and preview text 4-7 words, especially for mobile viewing.

**Why It Works**: Longer subject lines get cut off on mobile devices, losing their impact before the reader sees the full message.

**Application**:
- Write your subject line
- Edit down to 4-7 words maximum
- Test on mobile to verify display

**Example**: "Oprah loved this" (4 words)

---

### ES-011: No Repeated Copy Rule

**The Rule**: Don't use identical copy in your subject line and email headline.

**Why It Works**: Repeated copy signals laziness and creates a negative brand association. Readers notice and feel less valued.

**Application**:
- Write subject line and headline separately
- Each should add new information or perspective
- Use same theme but different phrasing

**Anti-Pattern**:
```
BAD:
Subject: "This isn't your average granola"
Headline: "This isn't your average granola"

GOOD:
Subject: "This isn't your average granola"
Headline: "Why 50,000 customers switched"
```

## BRAND & DESIGN CONSISTENCY

### ES-014: Consistent Brand Design Rule

**The Rule**: Email design should be an extension of your website and ads - same visual language, colors, typography.

**Why It Works**: Consistency builds trust and recognition. Mismatched design creates cognitive dissonance and reduces brand recall.

**Application**:
- Use the same brand guidelines for emails as for your website and advertising
- Match colors, fonts, imagery style
- Maintain voice consistency

**Example**: Magic Spoon's emails match their website and ads perfectly - same playful colors, typography, and tone.

---

### ES-022: Email Slice Size Limit

**The Rule**: When exporting email designs as images, keep slices under 1000px height and compress before uploading.

**Why It Works**: Large image files slow load times and get clipped. Smaller slices load faster and display correctly.

**Application**:
- Use Figma slice tool at max 1000px height
- Compress via iloveimg.com or similar before Klaviyo upload
- Test load speed on mobile networks

---

### ES-023: Graphic vs Text-Based Email Mix

**The Rule**: Mostly graphic-based emails for engagement, strategically mixed with plain text emails for conversion.

**Why It Works**: Graphics keep the list engaged visually. Text emails look like they're from a friend and convert at higher rates. The mix prevents pattern fatigue.

**Application**:
- Send mostly designed emails
- Add 2-4 plain text emails per month from a real person on the team
- Use text emails for personal moments (founder notes, abandoned cart follow-ups)

**Example**:
- Graphic email for product launch
- Text email: "Hey, quick reminder your discount expires tonight - June"

## FLOW STRUCTURES

### ES-015: Welcome Flow Structure

**The Rule**: 4-email welcome flow structure:
1. Email 1 (Immediate): Discount code + brief brand intro
2. Email 2 (Day 1): Brand story + founder message
3. Email 3 (Day 2): Us vs Them / Differentiators
4. Email 4 (Day 3): Last chance on discount + support offer

**Why It Works**: Progressive trust-building from transactional to emotional to urgency. Moves subscriber through awareness stages methodically.

**Application**:
- Set up automated welcome flow with 1-day delays between emails
- Each email has single focus
- Build relationship before pushing hard

---

### ES-016: Four Abandonment Flow Types

**The Rule**: Set up 4 separate abandonment flows:
1. Site Abandoned (visited but didn't view products)
2. Browse Abandoned (viewed product but didn't add to cart)
3. Cart Abandoned (added to cart but didn't checkout)
4. Checkout Abandoned (started checkout but didn't complete)

**Why It Works**: Each stage represents different intent level. Different messaging works for each. Most brands only have checkout abandoned - missing recovery opportunities.

**Application**: Create separate Klaviyo flows for each trigger type with appropriate messaging:
- Site abandoned: Bestsellers, browsing encouragement
- Browse abandoned: Viewed product + alternatives
- Cart abandoned: Cart contents + urgency
- Checkout abandoned: Cart contents + discount + support offer

---

### ES-017: Abandonment Email Timing

**The Rule**:
- Cart/Checkout abandonment: First email at 30 minutes
- Site abandonment: First email at 4 hours

**Why It Works**:
- Cart/checkout: Captures impulse recovery while purchase intent is still hot
- Site: Low-intent visitors need more time. They may come back on their own.

**Application**:
- Set first cart abandonment email to 30-minute delay
- Set first site abandonment email to 4-hour delay
- Subsequent emails spaced 1 day apart

---

### ES-024: Sending Frequency by Revenue

**The Rule**: Scale email frequency based on store size:
- $0-50k/month: 2x per week
- $50k-500k/month: 3x per week
- $500k-1M/month: 4x per week
- $1M+/month: 5-7x per week

**Why It Works**: Higher traffic = more new subscribers = can afford more unsubscribes = can send more frequently. Baseline is 3x per week as the sweet spot.

**Application**: Match your send frequency to your monthly revenue/traffic tier. Never go below 2x/week.
