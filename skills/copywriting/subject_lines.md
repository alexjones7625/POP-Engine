## Foundational Tactics

### Core Subject Line Rules
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-001
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3631-3827)
**Best For**: All email types
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Subject lines are the single most important element determining email revenue. Test them rigorously.

**Cardinal Rules:**
1. **Measure by REVENUE not opens** - Same email with different subject lines can have 3x difference in placed order rate
2. **Keep it short** - 2-5 words maximum
3. **Use Title Case** - Capitalize first letter of each word
4. **Build curiosity** - Don't reveal everything in subject line
5. **Limit emojis** - 1-2 maximum, at END of subject line only
6. **Don't use emojis in every email** - Loses impact

#### Why It Works
- Short = mobile-friendly, easy to scan
- Title Case = professional, stands out
- Curiosity = drives opens
- Revenue testing = actual business impact

#### Real Results
**Case Study (MaxYTvids.txt):**
- Same email content, different subject lines
- Subject A: 1.57% placed order rate
- Subject B: 0.5% placed order rate
- **3x difference in revenue from subject line alone**

### Length & Format Standards
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-002
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3631-3700)
**Best For**: All campaigns and flows
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
**Optimal Length:** 2-5 words

**Format Rules:**
- Title Case (Example: "A Taste Of Sunshine")
- No ALL CAPS (spam trigger)
- No excessive punctuation (!!!! spam trigger)
- Avoid spam words: FREE, BUY NOW, CLICK HERE

**Character Limits:**
- Mobile: ~30-40 characters visible
- Desktop: ~60-70 characters visible
- Target: 20-30 characters (2-5 words)

#### Examples - Good
- "A Taste of Sunshine" (curiosity)
- "Myth. Busted." (periods add suspense)
- "They're Finally Here" (anticipation)
- "It's Literally This Easy" (cocky confidence)
- "You're Going to Love This"
- "One More Day"

#### Examples - Avoid
- "AMAZING SALE - BUY NOW AND SAVE!!!" (spam triggers)
- "Our complete guide to finding the perfect protein powder for your fitness goals" (way too long)
- "hey check this out" (unprofessional, lowercase)

## Advanced Tactics

### Curiosity-Driven Subject Lines
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-003
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt, campaign-copywriting.md
**Best For**: Educational content, product highlights, story-driven campaigns
**Complexity**: Moderate

#### The Framework
Open a loop that can only be closed by reading the email.

**Structures:**
1. **Incomplete Statement**: "What We Just Discovered..."
2. **Question Hint**: "Is This Why...?"
3. **Vague Benefit**: "The Secret to [Outcome]"
4. **Number Tease**: "3 Things You Don't Know"
5. **Myth/Truth**: "The Truth About [Topic]"

#### Examples
- "Myth. Busted."
- "They're Finally Here"
- "You Won't Believe This"
- "The One Thing We Changed"
- "What Happened Next"
- "Is This Why?"

#### When to Use
- Mid-funnel educational emails
- Product announcements
- Story-based campaigns
- When you have strong content inside

#### When to Avoid
- If the payoff is weak (erodes trust)
- Transactional emails (order confirmations)
- When clarity > curiosity (urgent messages)
- Too frequently (becomes boy-who-cried-wolf)

### Benefit-Driven Subject Lines
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-004
**Source**: campaign-copywriting.md
**Best For**: Promotional emails, direct offers, bottom-of-funnel
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Lead with clear value proposition.

**Structures:**
1. **Direct Discount**: "20% Off Today"
2. **Free Shipping**: "Free Shipping Ends Tonight"
3. **Feature Callout**: "Pre-Digested Whey Inside"
4. **Outcome Promise**: "Wake Up With Energy"
5. **Convenience**: "Order Before 2pm"

#### Examples
- "20% Off Everything"
- "Free Shipping Today"
- "Your Favorites Are Back"
- "New Arrivals You'll Love"
- "Last Chance"

#### When to Use
- Sales and promotions
- Abandoned cart/checkout flows
- Customer winback (with offer)
- When you have strong incentive

#### When to Avoid
- Welcome flow Email 1 (too salesy)
- Educational content (undersells value)
- Brand storytelling emails
- When benefit isn't immediately credible

### Urgency Subject Lines
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-005
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt, campaign-frameworks.md
**Best For**: Sale closers, abandoned cart, limited offers
**Complexity**: Moderate (risk of overuse)

#### The Framework
Time-bound or scarcity-based action drivers.

**Types:**
1. **Time Scarcity**: "Ends Tonight"
2. **Quantity Scarcity**: "Almost Gone"
3. **Countdown**: "4 Hours Left"
4. **Last Chance**: "Final Call"
5. **Event Closure**: "Last Day"

#### Examples
**Time-Based:**
- "Ends Tonight"
- "Final Hours"
- "One More Day"
- "24 Hours Left"

**Quantity-Based:**
- "Almost Sold Out"
- "Limited Stock"
- "Last Few Left"

**Event-Based:**
- "Last Chance"
- "Final Call"
- "It's Over Tomorrow"

#### When to Use
- End of sales/promotions
- Flash sales
- Abandoned cart Email 3+
- Actually true urgency/scarcity

#### Critical Rule
**NEVER fake urgency.** Only use if deadline/scarcity is REAL. Fake urgency destroys brand trust permanently.

**When to Avoid:**
- Welcome flows
- Educational emails
- Every email (loses effectiveness)
- When there's no actual urgency

### Preview Text Strategy
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-006
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3700-3750)
**Best For**: All emails
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Preview text is the second subject line. Use it strategically.

**Rules:**
1. **1 sentence maximum**
2. **Regular capitalization** (not Title Case like subject)
3. **End with "..."** to open loop
4. **Extend curiosity** from subject line
5. **Don't repeat** subject line verbatim

#### Structure
- Subject Line: Create curiosity
- Preview Text: Deepen curiosity without revealing
- Both work together to drive open

#### Examples

**Example 1:**
- Subject: "They're Finally Here"
- Preview: "The wait is over, but there's a catch..."

**Example 2:**
- Subject: "Myth. Busted."
- Preview: "Everything you thought you knew about protein powder..."

**Example 3:**
- Subject: "One More Day"
- Preview: "After tomorrow, this disappears for good..."

**Example 4:**
- Subject: "20% Off Everything"
- Preview: "Including the bestsellers that never go on sale..."

#### Why It Works
- Gives you 2 chances to hook reader
- Preview text shows on mobile prominently
- "..." creates open loop (curiosity)
- Regular capitalization feels conversational

#### When to Avoid
- Don't leave blank (wasted opportunity)
- Don't just repeat subject (redundant)
- Don't reveal the whole story (kills curiosity)

## Testing Protocol

### Subject Line A/B Testing
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-007
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 4933-5051)
**Best For**: All campaigns
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Test subject lines weekly. Measure by REVENUE not opens.

**Test Setup:**
- 50/50 split
- Minimum 1,000 recipients per variant
- Run for 24 hours
- Measure: Placed order rate (revenue), NOT open rate

**What to Test:**
1. Curiosity vs Benefit-Driven
2. Short (2-3 words) vs Medium (4-5 words)
3. With emoji vs Without emoji
4. Question vs Statement
5. Urgency vs Non-urgency
6. Specific vs Vague

#### Why Revenue Not Opens
- Opens don't pay bills
- Subject line that gets opens but no sales = failure
- Subject line that gets fewer opens but more orders = winner
- Real business impact = revenue

#### Testing Cadence
- Test 1 subject line per campaign
- Document winners in performance log
- Build library of proven subject lines
- Iterate on winners

#### Example Test
**Variant A**: "The Secret to Better Sleep"
- Opens: 28%
- Click rate: 3.2%
- Placed order rate: 0.8%

**Variant B**: "3 Sleep Hacks"
- Opens: 22%
- Click rate: 4.1%
- Placed order rate: 1.4%

**Winner**: Variant B (1.75x more revenue despite lower opens)

## Emoji Usage Guidelines

### Emoji Strategy
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-008
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3680-3700)
**Best For**: Select campaigns
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Use emojis sparingly for visual differentiation.

**Rules:**
1. **Maximum 1-2 emojis** per subject line
2. **Place at END** of subject line (not beginning)
3. **Don't use in every email** - loses impact
4. **Match brand voice** - casual brands only
5. **Test vs no emoji** - not always better

#### Examples - Good Usage
- "One More Day ⏰"
- "They're Back ✨"
- "Summer Ready ☀️"
- "Almost Gone 🔥"

#### Examples - Bad Usage
- "🔥🔥🔥 SALE ALERT 🔥🔥🔥" (excessive)
- "💰 Free Shipping" (emoji at start)
- Every single email has emoji (overuse)

#### When to Use
- Seasonal campaigns (☀️ 🎃 ❄️)
- Urgency messages (⏰ 🔥)
- Celebration/milestones (🎉)
- Casual brand voice

#### When to Avoid
- B2B or professional brands
- Every email (dilutes impact)
- Serious/empathetic messages
- When testing shows it hurts performance

**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-011
**Source**: flow-winback.md, MaxYTvids (1).txt

**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Acknowledge absence without guilt, provide value-based reason to return.

**Structures:**

2. **What's New**: "A Lot Has Changed"
3. **Miss You**: "We Miss You"
4. **Exclusive Value**: "Something Just For You"
5. **Last Attempt**: "Before You Go..."

#### Examples
- "We Miss You"
- "It's Been a While"
- "Come Back? Here's 20% Off"
- "Are You Still There?"
- "One Last Thing..."

## Seasonal & Holiday Subjects

### Holiday Subject Lines
**Tactic ID**: SUBJ-012
**Source**: campaign-frameworks.md
**Best For**: Holiday campaigns
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Tie into holidays naturally, don't force it.

**Major Holidays:**
- Mother's Day: "Celebrate Mom"
- Memorial Day: "Memorial Day Sale"
- 4th of July: "Independence Day Savings"
- Labor Day: "End of Summer Sale"
- Black Friday: "BFCM Starts Now"
- Cyber Monday: "Cyber Monday Extended"

**Random Holidays (use checkiday.com):**
- National Coffee Day
- Random Acts of Kindness Day
- Pi Day (3/14)

#### Examples
- "Happy Mother's Day ❤️"
- "Memorial Day Starts Now"
- "BFCM: 30% Off Everything"
- "National [X] Day Sale"
