## PSYCH-002: FOMO/Urgency Trigger

### Why It Works
Urgency shifts decision-making from analytical to emotional. When customers feel they need to act now, they bypass overthinking.

### Effective Urgency Language
```
"Ends tonight"
"Last chance"
"Only a few hours left"
"Final day"
"Closing soon"
"Your window is closing"
```

### FOMO Tactics for Ecommerce
| Tactic | When to Use |
|--------|-------------|
| Limited edition products | Product launches |
| Flash sales with real end times | Revenue spikes |
| Exclusive early access | VIP segments |
| Low stock alerts (if true) | Product pages, cart emails |
| Member-only windows | Loyalty programs |

### Critical Warning
**Fake urgency kills trust.** Running a "flash sale" every other day trains customers to ignore you.

## PSYCH-003: Social Proof Hierarchy

### Ranked by Power (Most to Least)
1. Video testimonials from relatable customers
2. Photo reviews with product images
3. Written reviews with specific details
4. Star ratings and review counts
5. "X people bought this" notifications
6. Media logos ("As seen in...")

### Key Insight
**A single authentic review does more for conversion than a paragraph of polished marketing copy.**

### Email Applications
```
- Include 1-2 testimonials in campaigns
- Show review counts for featured products
- Highlight UGC (user-generated content)
- Reference community size ("Join 50,000+ members")
- Share customer transformation stories
```

## PSYCH-004: Personalization Tactics

|-------|---------|
| Basic | "Hi {first_name}" |

## PSYCH-011: Reciprocity Principle

### The Rule
When you give something, people feel compelled to give back.

### Email Applications
| Give | Then Ask |
|------|----------|
| Valuable content | Purchase |
| Discount to loyal customers | Referral |
| Helpful tips (no selling) | Review |
| Unexpected bonus | Upsell |

### Implementation
Provide value for emails 1-3, then make the ask in email 4+.

## PSYCH-014: Reverse Psychology Subject Lines

### Quantified Impact
**Reverse psychology subject lines boost opens by 20%+**

### Patterns
```
"This might not apply to you"
"Nothing important here"

## PSYCH-016: Benefits vs Features

### Cognitive Load Rule
The brain can only process limited information. Features require work to understand relevance. Benefits trigger immediate emotional responses.

### Transformation
| Feature | Benefit |
|---------|---------|
| "Machine washable, fade-resistant" | "Sleep better than ever on ultra-soft comfort" |
| "10,000 mAh battery" | "Never run out of power again" |
| "24/7 support" | "Help whenever you need it" |
| "AI-powered" | "Saves you 10 hours a week" |

### Rule
Lead with benefit, support with feature. Subject lines and above-the-fold = pure benefit. Features in supporting copy.

## PSYCH-018: Real vs Fake Urgency

### Real Urgency (Use These)
| Type | Example |
|------|---------|
| Real deadlines | "Sale ends Dec 31 at midnight" |
| Actual limited inventory | "Only 12 left in your size" |
| True seasonal moments | "Valentine's shipping cutoff" |
| Authentic product drops | "New collection launches Friday" |

### Fake Urgency (Never Use)
- Evergreen "flash sales"
- Fake countdown timers that reset
- "Limited time" that never ends
- Manufactured scarcity

### The Test
If customers figure out it's fake, they'll never trust you again.

## PSYCH-021: Trust Builders

### Elements for Email
```
- Professional design (consistent branding)
- Clear sender identity (recognizable from name)
- Easy unsubscribe (visible, works)
- Privacy respect (don't overshare)
