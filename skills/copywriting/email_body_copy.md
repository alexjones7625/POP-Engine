## Foundational Tactics

### SCE Framework (Core Principles)
**Tactic ID**: BODY-001
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3141-3400)
**Best For**: All email body copy
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Three Pillars
All email body copy must be:
1. **Skimmable**
2. **Clear & Concise**
3. **Engaging**

#### Critical Context
**Customers spend MAX 3-5 seconds looking at each email**
- Customer IQ drops 70% when in inbox ("zombie mode")
- They skim, don't read
- Must deliver value in seconds

#### Why It Works
- Respects limited attention
- Matches actual user behavior
- Increases conversion by reducing friction

### Skimmable Formatting
**Tactic ID**: BODY-002
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3141-3250)
**Best For**: All campaigns and flows
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Make it possible to understand the entire email in 3 seconds without reading every word.

**Core Rules:**
1. **No blocks of text** - Use line breaks liberally
2. **Bold the main points** - Readers jump to bolded text
3. **Headlines allow full picture** - Should be able to read only headlines and understand
4. **Short paragraphs** - 2-3 sentences maximum
5. **Visual hierarchy** - Headlines > subheads > body

#### Structure Template
```
[HEADLINE - Bold, large]

Brief intro sentence.

[SUBHEAD 1 - Bold]
2-3 sentences of explanation.

[SUBHEAD 2 - Bold]
2-3 sentences of explanation.

[CTA - Button]
```

#### Examples - Good
```
**Wake Up With More Energy**

No more 3pm crashes. No jitters.

**What Makes It Work**
Pre-digested whey absorbs 3x faster than regular protein.

**What You'll Feel**
Energy within 30 minutes. Focus for hours.

[Shop Now]
```

#### Examples - Avoid
```
Hey there! We wanted to tell you about our amazing new protein powder that we've been working on for months. It has pre-digested whey protein isolate which is different from regular whey because it absorbs faster and doesn't cause bloating. We've tested it with hundreds of customers and they all report feeling more energy and less crashes throughout the day. Plus it comes in three delicious flavors...

[Giant paragraph continues for 10 more lines]
```

#### When to Use
- Every email (non-negotiable)
- Especially important on mobile
- Critical for campaigns (less so for text-based founder notes)

### Clear & Concise Writing
**Tactic ID**: BODY-003
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3251-3320)
**Best For**: All email types
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
**One takeaway per email.** If you try to communicate 5 things, they remember none.

**Rules:**
1. **Single focus** - One product, one benefit, one message
2. **1-2 scrolls maximum** - Email should fit in 1-2 phone screen heights
3. **Short and sweet wins** - Less is more
4. **No unnecessary info** - Every sentence must earn its place

#### Why One Takeaway
- 100% attention on 1 thing > 20% attention on 5 things
- Customer in "zombie mode" can't process complexity
- Single message = higher recall and action

#### Examples - Single Takeaway

**Good - Email about pre-digested whey:**
```
**Absorbs 3x Faster**

Regular whey sits in your stomach for hours.
Pre-digested whey absorbs in minutes.

That's why you feel the difference immediately.

[Try It Now]
```

**Bad - Too many takeaways:**
```
**Our Protein Has Everything**

- Pre-digested whey (absorbs faster)
- No artificial sweeteners (clean ingredients)
- 5 delicious flavors (chocolate, vanilla, strawberry, banana, cookies & cream)
- Mixes easily (no clumps)
- Great price (cheaper than competitors)
- Made in USA (quality guaranteed)
- Subscribe and save (10% off)

[Shop Now]
```

**Why Bad Example Fails:**
- Reader doesn't know what to focus on
- No clear differentiation
- Diluted message = diluted impact

#### What to Cut
- Unnecessary pleasantries ("We hope this email finds you well")
- Obvious statements ("You'll get exclusive offers by being on our list" - duh)
- Filler words ("really", "very", "just", "actually")
- Redundant explanations

### Engaging Content Tactics
**Tactic ID**: BODY-004
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3321-3400)
**Best For**: Educational and relationship emails
**Complexity**: Moderate

#### The Framework
Must trigger dopamine. Use:
1. **Education** - Teach something valuable
2. **Humor** - Make them smile
3. **Punchy phrasing** - Short but powerful
4. **Emotional triggers** - Connect to feelings
5. **Helpful info** - Solve a problem

#### What is "Punchy"
- Short sentences
- Dense information
- Full of power
- Zero fluff

**Example - Punchy:**
"You're tired. Not lazy. There's a difference."

**Example - Not Punchy:**
"Many people who feel tired throughout the day often think they're being lazy, but actually there might be an underlying issue."

#### Educational Content
Give information people can actually use.

**Examples:**
- How to use the product effectively
- Tips related to product category
- Common mistakes to avoid
- Industry insights

**Food brands:** Recipe content performs incredibly well

#### Humor
Make them smile, not laugh-out-loud.

**Examples:**
- Self-deprecating brand humor
- Relatable observations
- Light wordplay
- Cultural references (test with audience)

#### Emotional Triggers
(See `skills/copywriting/emotional_triggers.md` for full list)

Common emotional hooks:
- Aspiration (who they want to be)
- Pain avoidance (what they want to avoid)
- Social proof (others like them)
- Exclusivity (special treatment)

## Advanced Tactics

### Infographic Integration
**Tactic ID**: BODY-005
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3401-3474)
**Best For**: All graphic-based emails
**Complexity**: Moderate (requires design)

#### The "Secret Sauce"
**At least 1 infographic per email. Avoid ALL blocks of copy.**

#### 9 Types of Infographics

| Type | Best For | Example Use |
|------|----------|-------------|
| 1. Checklists | Showing benefits creatively | "✓ More energy ✓ Better focus ✓ No crash" |
| 2. Icon Graphics | Visual benefits, easy skim | Icons representing each benefit |
| 3. Feature Diagrams | Point to product features | Arrows pointing to product parts |
| 4. Timelines | Customer journey, results timing | "Day 1 → Week 1 → Month 1" |
| 5. Numbered Lists | Brain differentiates easily | "3 Reasons Why..." |
| 6. Comparison Charts | Us vs Them, product tiers | Side-by-side comparison table |
| 7. Tables | Spread info into sections | Ingredient breakdown |
| 8. Flowcharts | Use cases, decision trees | "Which product is right for you?" |
| 9. Graphs | Visualize statistics | Bar chart showing results |

#### Why Infographics Work
- Visual processing is 60,000x faster than text
- Skimmable in seconds
- Memorable
- Mobile-friendly
- Professional appearance

#### When to Use Each Type

**Checklists:**
- Welcome emails (what you get)
- Product benefit highlights
- Guarantee/risk reversals

**Icon Graphics:**
- Feature callouts
- Benefits overview
- "What's included" sections

**Timelines:**
- Post-purchase (when to expect results)
- Welcome flow (what happens next)
- Product development story

**Numbered Lists:**
- "3 reasons why..."
- "5 ways to use..."
- Step-by-step instructions

**Comparison Charts:**
- Us vs Them emails
- Product tier comparison
- Before vs After

**Graphs:**
- Third-party research
- Customer results
- Performance data

#### Design Tool
**Use Figma, not Canva** - More capabilities for professional email design

### Mobile-First Design
**Tactic ID**: BODY-006
**Source**: Email Anatomy.txt, MaxYTvids.txt
**Best For**: All emails
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
70%+ of emails are opened on mobile. Design for mobile first.

**Mobile Requirements:**
1. **Button above fold** - Visible without scrolling
2. **Large thumb-friendly buttons** - 2+ inches wide minimum
3. **Center-aligned buttons** - Easy to tap, no thumb stretching
4. **Short paragraphs** - 2-3 sentences max
5. **Compressed images** - Fast load speed
6. **White space** - Don't cram content

#### Button Best Practices
**CRO Checklist:**
- ✓ Button above the fold (visible immediately)
- ✓ 2+ inches wide for easy tapping
- ✓ Center-aligned (ergonomic for thumbs)
- ✓ Clear, repeating CTAs (2-3 buttons through email)
- ✓ Button per product shown
- ✓ General "Shop All" CTA at bottom
- ✓ High contrast color (stands out from background)

#### Common Mobile Mistakes
- ❌ Tiny buttons (hard to tap)
- ❌ Left-aligned buttons (thumb has to stretch)
- ❌ Only one CTA at bottom (missed opportunity)
- ❌ Long paragraphs (unreadable on small screen)
- ❌ No white space (overwhelming)

### Text-Based Email Strategy
**Tactic ID**: BODY-007
**Source**: MaxYTvids (1).txt (lines 290-310), campaign-strategy.md
**Best For**: Founder notes, personal messages, winback
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
Send 2-3 text-based emails per month minimum. They feel personal and build connection.

**What is "Text-Based":**
- No graphics (or minimal branding)
- Plain text body
- Looks like email from a friend
- Can be built right in Klaviyo (no design needed)

**Two Types:**

**1. Branded Text-Based:**
- Has logo at top
- Plain text body
- Standard footer
- Feels semi-personal

**2. Plain-Plain:**
- Nothing but text
- Native inbox format
- Reply-to enabled
- Feels like 1:1 email

#### When to Use
- Founder notes in welcome flow (Email 2)
- Abandoned cart personal nudge (Email 2)
- Winback "are you okay?" check-in
- Apology/explanation emails
- VIP/personal outreach
- Post-purchase thank you (Email 1)

#### Structure Template
```
Subject: [Personal, conversational]

Hey [First Name],

[2-3 sentences of personal message]

[Brief value statement or question]

[Call to action - can be subtle]

[Signature]
[Name]
[Title, Brand]

P.S. [Often where the offer/urgency lives]
```

#### Example - Post-Purchase Thank You
```
Subject: Thank you

Hey Sarah,

Just wanted to send a quick note to say thanks for your order.

We packed it up this morning and it's heading your way.

If you have any questions before it arrives, just hit reply.

Thanks again,
John
Founder, [Brand]

P.S. You can add to your order in the next 2 hours if you forgot anything - just reply to this email.
```

#### Why It Works
- Breaks pattern of graphic emails
- Feels personal (not mass marketing)
- Higher engagement rates
- Builds founder/brand connection
- Lower cost (no design time)

## Anti-Patterns (What NOT to Do)

### Common Email Body Mistakes
**Tactic ID**: BODY-008
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 3350-3400)
**Best For**: Avoiding common failures
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The "Do Not" List

**❌ 1. Blocks of Text**
- Unreadable on mobile
- Impossible to skim
- Looks like homework
- Instant delete

**❌ 2. Too Many Takeaways**
- Trying to communicate 5 things = they remember none
- Diluted focus = diluted results
- If everything is important, nothing is important

**❌ 3. Boring Content**
- Cannot bore people into a purchase (David Ogilvy)
- Educational doesn't mean boring
- Must trigger dopamine

**❌ 4. Unnecessary Information**
- "You'll get exclusive offers" (they know)
- "We value your privacy" (table stakes)
- "Here's what to expect" (just show them)
- Filler that doesn't drive action

**❌ 5. No Value Proposition**
- "Check out our new product" (why?)
- "Shop our sale" (what's in it for me?)
- Must answer "What do I get?"

**❌ 6. Weak Headlines**
- Headlines should tell the story alone
- Don't waste headline space on obvious statements
- Make headlines work hard

**❌ 7. Hidden CTAs**
- Buried at bottom
- Tiny text links
- No visual hierarchy
- Make CTA obvious and repeating

**❌ 8. Dash Separators (AI Tell)**
- Never use " - " as a sentence connector or clause separator
- Reads as AI-generated. Real copywriters use periods, commas, or restructure the sentence
- Bad: "Premium microfiber fabric - not rubber, not foam"
- Good: "Premium microfiber fabric. Not rubber, not foam."
- Bad: "Built for the beach - the pool - the lake"
- Good: "Built for the beach, the pool, the lake."
- Compound words with hyphens are fine (patent-pending, game-like, side-by-side)

**❌ 9. Repeating Features Across Sections**
- Never restate the same product specs in consecutive sections
- If body copy mentions "patent-pending silicone grip" then the product section should NOT repeat it
- Each section should advance the pitch, not echo the last one
- Body copy = introduce the feature. Product section = sell the benefit/experience. Closing = tie it together
- Bad: Body says "silicone grip strips" then product section says "silicone grip strips" again
- Good: Body says "silicone grip strips" then product section says "the grip that makes every throw feel like game day"

**❌ 10. Oxford Commas**
- NEVER use oxford commas in any client email copy
- Write: "hockey, boxing, lacrosse and football"
- NOT: "hockey, boxing, lacrosse, and football"
- This applies to ALL clients, ALL emails, no exceptions

## Content Mix Strategy

### 70/30 Rule
**Tactic ID**: BODY-009
**Source**: MaxYTvids (2).txt, campaign-strategy.md
**Best For**: Campaign planning, content calendar
**Complexity**: Simple

#### The Framework
**70-80% Educational/Value Content**
**20-30% Promotional Content**

**Why:**
- Nobody wants only offers and discounts
- Seems needy
- Creates sales resistance
- Being altruistic positions you as sought-after brand

#### Formula
**Education + Positioning = Sales Without Discounts**

#### Educational Content Types
- How to use product effectively
- Industry tips and tricks
- Common mistakes to avoid
- Behind the scenes
- Product development story
- Founder's story
- Customer transformation stories
- Research/science behind product
- FAQ deep-dives

#### Promotional Content Types
- Sales and discounts
- Limited offers
- New product launches
- Back in stock alerts
- Flash sales

#### Balance Example (Month)
16 emails sent:
- 11 educational/value (69%)
- 5 promotional (31%)

**Result:** Higher engagement, less unsubscribes, sales without constant discounting

## ChatGPT Integration

### AI-Assisted Copywriting Protocol
**Tactic ID**: BODY-010
**Source**: MaxYTvids.txt (lines 5052-5177)
**Best For**: Speeding up copy creation
**Complexity**: Moderate

#### Step 1: Prime ChatGPT (Once Per Thread)
```
I'm going to give you background information on my ecom brand. Process it so I can ask you to write email copy later.

My ecommerce brand is [name] and we sell [products].
Our mission and goal is [mission].
Our brand is unique because [USPs].
Our products are unique because [product differentiators].
My customer is ages [range] with [demographics]. They enjoy [interests].
Brand voice: [describe tone - conversational, scientific, playful, etc.]
Customer goals and aspirations: [what they want to achieve]
Also here is my website: [URL]
```

#### Step 2: Request Specific Email Copy
```
We are creating a graphic-based email campaign with minimal copy to send to our customers. The topic is about [topic] and [key selling points].

Using the information I gave you, write me:
- A quick and punchy headline
- A slightly longer sub-headline
- 2-3 sentences of body copy leading to a shop now CTA
```

#### Step 3: Iterate and Humanize
**CRITICAL RULE: NEVER use ChatGPT output as-is.**

ChatGPT provides starting point. You must:
- Add brand voice
- Cut fluff
- Make it punchier
- Add personality
- Inject emotion
- Test variations

#### Micro-Topic Generation Prompt
```
I have a brand in [niche] that sells [products]. Our brand is meant to [mission]. Our average customer is [demographics + psychographics].

Give me overarching topics my brand can create content about. Topics should be general and tie into product benefits and customer lives.

Then create subtopics under each. Subtopics should be specific, educate the customer, and position our products as a solution.
```

**Output Example:**
```
Topic: Morning Routines
- Subtopic: Why your first 30 minutes determines your whole day
- Subtopic: The protein timing window most people miss
- Subtopic: Coffee before protein - mistake or myth?
```

### Us vs Them Email Template
**Tactic ID**: BODY-013
**Source**: campaign-frameworks.md (Framework #10)
**Best For**: Differentiation, competitive positioning
**Complexity**: Moderate

#### Structure
```
Subject: What Makes Us Different

[HEADLINE: "How We're Different"]

[Confident intro - not defensive or needy]

[COMPARISON TABLE/INFOGRAPHIC]

Our Brand | Other Brands
-----------|-------------
Feature 1  | Generic
Feature 2  | Missing
Feature 3  | Inferior

[Brief explanation of why differences matter]

[CTA: "Choose Quality"]

[Optional: Risk reversal - try us risk-free]

[CTA: "Shop Now"]
```

#### Tone Guidelines
- ✓ Confident, not cocky
- ✓ Factual, not attacking
- ✓ Focus on YOUR strengths, not their weaknesses
- ✗ Never directly name competitors (unless allowed legally)
- ✗ Don't sound desperate or defensive
- ✗ Avoid negativity

**Tactic ID**: BODY-014

**Complexity**: Simple

```

### Before vs After Email Template
**Tactic ID**: BODY-015
**Source**: campaign-frameworks.md (Framework #17)
**Best For**: Transformation products, results-driven
**Complexity**: Moderate (need good visuals)

#### Structure
```
Subject: See The Difference

[HEADLINE: "Real Results"]

[Customer name and timeframe]
"[Name]'s transformation in [X weeks]"

[Brief quote from customer]

[What they did - simple explanation]

## New Tactics from Extractions

### The 3-Second Rule
**Tactic ID**: BODY-016
**Source**: copywriting_pdf.md

#### The Rule
In 2025, you have approximately 3 seconds of customer attention per email (down from 5-10 seconds previously).

#### Why It Works
Social media has drastically reduced attention spans. Customers check email in quick bursts between tasks - walking to bathroom, waiting for toast, between meetings.

#### Application
Design every element of your email to communicate within 3 seconds. If they can't get your point immediately, they leave.

---

### The Sales Filter Test
**Tactic ID**: BODY-017
**Source**: copywriting_pdf.md

#### The Rule
For every sentence you write, ask yourself: "Does this push the customer closer to a sale?"

#### Why It Works
Eliminates fluff and unnecessary copy that wastes precious attention. Every word must earn its place.

#### Application
Review each line of copy. If it doesn't advance the sale, delete it.

#### Example
Welcome emails that list "exclusive offers, insights, sneak peeks, community" are wasted space - subscribers already know this.

---

### The Zombie Brain Principle
**Tactic ID**: BODY-018
**Source**: copywriting_pdf.md

#### The Rule
Customer IQ drops approximately 50% the second they open their email inbox. They're in "zombie brain mode."

#### Why It Works
Email checking is a low-cognitive-effort activity done on autopilot. Nobody reads email with focused attention.

#### Application
Write for a reader who is mentally checked out. Ultra-simple language, obvious benefits, clear next steps.

#### Test
If your message requires thought to understand, it won't be understood.

---

### The Dopamine Trigger Rule
**Tactic ID**: BODY-019
**Source**: copywriting_pdf.md

#### The Rule
Emails must trigger dopamine in the customer's brain to create positive brand association.

#### Why It Works
Dopamine release creates positive association, leading to future opens and sales. Customers crave dopamine from TikTok/Instagram - your emails compete with this.

#### Application
Include elements that provide instant gratification:
- Humor
- Surprise
- Useful facts
- Visual interest
- Something new/novel

---

### The Education Engagement Rule
**Tactic ID**: BODY-020
**Source**: copywriting_pdf.md

#### The Rule
If someone learns something new every time they open your email, you're in a good place.

#### Why It Works
People love learning and want to make educated purchasing decisions. Facts create engagement and trust.

#### Application
Include one interesting fact or piece of useful information in every email.

#### Example
Magic Mind email teaching about Golden Root/Rhodiola Rosea and its 140+ active compounds.

---

### PS Section for Key Info
**Tactic ID**: BODY-021
**Source**: max_video_transcript.md

#### The Rule
Put critical information (support offer, deadline) in a PS section at the end of text emails.

#### Why It Works
Everybody reads the PS. It stands out from the body copy. It's a reliable attention anchor.

#### Application
```
[Body copy]

[Signature]
[Name]

P.S. If you have any questions, just reply to this email and our team will respond ASAP
```

#### Best Uses
- Support offer
- Deadline reminder
- Secondary CTA
- Personal touch
- Urgency amplifier
